⌁ research

Game-Theoretic and AI-Based Modeling of Depression in Digital and Social Environments — an academic research paper, ~15–20 pages.

Depression affects an estimated 280 million people worldwide, with prevalence rising sharply among young adults and adolescents since the COVID-19 pandemic. As platforms like Twitter, Reddit, and Instagram have become central to how people express and experience mental health struggles, they've generated continuous behavioral signals that traditional episodic clinical assessment methods struggle to capture.

This paper reviews 30 peer-reviewed studies published between 2013–2023 at the intersection of AI, natural language processing, and game-theoretic modeling for depression detection, and identifies a consistent gap: models that perform well on curated datasets degrade sharply on noisy, real-world, linguistically diverse data.

To close this gap, the paper proposes GT-AID (Game-Theoretic and AI-based Depression detection) — a unified framework combining noise-resilient preprocessing, transformer and recurrent neural models, and game-theoretic interaction modeling for both detection and intervention. Experiments show GT-AID holds up far more stably across real-world conditions than existing models, which lose significant accuracy outside curated datasets. Simulations on a synthetic social network further suggest that structured interventions — particularly peer-based alerts — can meaningfully reduce patterns of social withdrawal, underscoring the value of combining behavioral analysis with social interaction modeling for practical mental health AI systems.
